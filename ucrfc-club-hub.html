UCRFC Club Hub

A website and season hub for an amateur rugby club: register, pay dues, and answer for Saturday in one place.

Company
Year2026
PlatformWeb

Context

An amateur rugby club runs on group chats, spreadsheets, and a coach chasing people for waivers. The Club Hub gives Union County Mudturtles RFC one screen for the thing that actually matters each week: who is playing on Saturday.

Problem

Availability, paperwork, schedule, and results each lived somewhere different. Selection depended on a coach reconciling all of it by hand, and players had no single place to see where they stood.

Role & Approach

A Finrez engagement covering the club’s brand guidelines, its public website, season dues through Stripe, text and email alerts, and the Club Hub. The website is the front door for anyone new: who the club is, when it plays, how to join. The hub is where members live once they have. Each one asks only for what its visitor came to do, and the two hand off at sign-up and dues.

The website

The latest round rebuilt the home page around match day. The hero opens on a slow push-in over the squad, with drifting fog and scroll parallax, the club’s numbers along the bottom and a news ticker under them. League schedule is a single row of match cards that scrolls sideways, with the next card peeking in on a phone.

The home page hero: one call to action, the club’s numbers and a news ticker.

League schedule and announcements.

Type

Headlines moved to Orbitron Black with a same-color stroke that makes the face heavier than the font ships, and body text to Barlow, self-hosted. H2s scaled up, buttons took the same stroke, and every page now ends on the home page footer.

Registration

Joining used to be a link to a payment page. It is now two steps on every page of the site: choose a division and team, say who is playing and how to reach you, then pay season dues. Dues are $500 a season through Stripe, and a small banner holds back until a visitor has scrolled past the first section before it asks.

Step one of two: division, team, and who is playing.

What dues cover, said plainly next to the price.

Alerts

Game-day updates, cancellations and club news go out by text and email. The sign-up asks for a number or an address and a team — youth, men’s, women’s or the whole club — so a parent of a U12 player doesn’t get the men’s side’s weather calls.

Alerts, by team.

The hub

The hub now has real accounts: sign-up with email confirmation, password reset and sessions that persist. One account covers a household, so a parent registers every child from the same login. Anyone who just wants to look can open a demo account.

One account per household.

Process

The dashboard is organized around match week. The fixture leads — opponent, kickoff, ground, and arrival time — and the availability question sits directly under it as three buttons: In, Maybe, Out. Social proof (“twenty-two others have answered”) and a stated selection deadline do the chasing that a coach used to do manually. Around it sit the player’s own season, the last result, the next three fixtures with inline availability, and a paperwork checklist that shows what is on file against what is due. Outstanding dues sit in a bar across the top with one button to pay.

Match week, from the player’s side.

Roles

The same hub changes with who signs in. Players see their own season. Coaches get squad status — who’s available, who hasn’t answered, training attendance — and can nudge everyone unanswered at once. Admins see the whole club: registrations by program, compliance, dues collected and outstanding, and a composer that sends an alert by push and SMS.

Coach: squad status before Thursday’s selection.

Admin: the whole club on one screen.

Roster

The roster shows numbers, positions, caps and paperwork status. Player photos are cropped square and shrunk in the browser, kept in private storage behind one-hour links, and only show to teammates if the family ticked the photo release.

The roster, filterable by forwards, backs and paperwork due.

Dues and data

Paying opens Stripe Checkout. The price lives in the database, never in the browser, and a signed webhook from Stripe marks a player paid, or unpaid again after a full refund. Row-level security means a player only ever reads their own registration; coaches and admins read the rest, and no browser can change a role or mark dues paid.

Paperwork: what’s on file and what’s owed.

User Journey

A parent finds the club on the website, picks a team and sends their details, then pays dues. On the hub they create one account for the household, finish each child’s registration, add a photo, and from then on the week comes down to one tap: In.

Constraints

The club is run by volunteers, which sets the ceiling on everything. Nothing here can need a paid administrator, so the hub connects to its services with one script in about fifteen minutes, runs on free tiers at a club this size, and until it’s connected falls back to the demo instead of breaking. Money is handled by Stripe, which a treasurer can audit without the club holding any of it. And a player’s side has to work on a phone, one-handed, in the two minutes between a notification and putting the phone away — the real constraint the availability question is designed against.

Summary

The website turns a visitor into a registered player in two steps, and the hub turns a season’s worth of admin into one dashboard where the weekly question is a single tap.

Next Steps

The website’s latest round shipped November 1. In the hub, accounts, registrations, dues and photos are built and run on the demo until the club connects Supabase and Stripe. Schedule, availability, messages and standings still show sample data, and are next.
